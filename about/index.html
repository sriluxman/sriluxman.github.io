I know what industrial software has to survive. I have developed and commissioned machines with it.

I am Sri, a product and technology leader in industrial automation and Technical Product Manager for next-generation industrial software at ABB. My work sits where machines, platforms and product strategy meet.

What I do

I take industrial problems that are still vague and future-facing, and help turn them into strategy, platforms and products that engineering teams can deliver and customers can rely on.

Today that means next-generation industrial software. I was instrumental in helping develop its vision together with product leadership, and as Technical Product Manager I now lead the way from abstract vision to tangible, release-ready product, as technical lead of a mid-size, geographically distributed team responsible for foundational platform services. The work is product judgment, architecture, and holding a distributed organization to an honest definition of done.

My first move with an unclear vision is to make it visible. A reference architecture and a working mockup settle more in a week than a quarter of slide reviews, because people can finally point at something. I dream big, experiment, fail fast and increment, and I hold just as firmly that agility does not mean chaos. Requirements engineering and architecture are the harness that keeps creative energy pulling in one direction.

Where my energy goes

Agentic and physical AI for the factory floor. The question that occupies me is a leadership question before it is a technical one: what has to be true before a factory lets an AI work beside its people and machines?

My answer is that trust decides the pace of adoption. An agent needs an identity and a defined authority, the machine's own rules need the final word, every action needs a record, and when the AI is unsure the machine must simply keep working as it does today. The model is the smallest part of that picture. I write about these convictions in the essays.

How I got here

Product

2024 to today, Technical Product Manager

From a vision that started abstract to a product ready for release, working across UX, architecture, R&D and global engineering teams, and translating customer and engineering needs into requirements people can build against.

Platform engineering and DevOps strategy

2021 to 2024, Product Owner, ABB

I worked with DevOps experts across the organization on platform engineering and DevOps strategy for R&D product and service teams: less duplicated effort, more reuse of what already worked, capability gaps closed, and real pull for shift-left practices. The approach was meaningful harmonization instead of forced standardization, and it contributed to the start of a central DevOps strategy unit that later grew significantly. It taught me how to change the way many teams work without authority over any of them.

Test and release management

2015 to 2021, Project Leader, ABB

I owned test and release strategy for mechatronic software products: durable, platform-agnostic automation across static and dynamic testing, and the standing bridge between engineering, product and customer-facing teams. It taught me that quality is a product feature.

Application engineering

2009 to 2015, International Application Engineer, B&R

I started close to machines and customers, working with OEMs and system integrators on complex machine applications, advanced motion in particular, from requirements through commissioning to series readiness, and teaching at the Automation Academy. It gave me what no later role can: physical respect for what industrial software has to survive outside the slide deck.

Where this is heading

I am inspired by the next scale of responsibility: leading product management, or product and engineering direction, for industrial software. The portfolios, the teams, and the connection between strategy and engineering reality. I am deliberate about preparing for it, by taking on the accountability within reach and by building the judgment that scale demands.

That ambition grows from a good place. I value the work and the people around me today, and strong next chapters are usually built on chapters that are going well. Industrial automation is rebuilding itself around software-defined systems and AI, and I want to carry real responsibility in that rebuild.

Why I still build

Away from the roadmap I keep a small lab of running experiments in agentic engineering, edge hardware and physical computing. It is how I keep product judgment calibrated against what is actually possible, and why my views on AI come from working systems.
