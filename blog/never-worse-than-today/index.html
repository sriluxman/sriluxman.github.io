Never worse than today

The design rule that makes AI acceptable in production: when the intelligence is unsure or unavailable, the machine behaves exactly as it does now.

Sri Prasanna MG, November 4, 2026

A factory manager does not need AI to be brilliant. A factory manager needs to know what happens on the day it is wrong.

Most AI proposals in industry stumble here. They describe the upside in detail and leave the failure case to a sentence about human oversight. People who run production hear the gap immediately, and they are right to.

There is a design rule that closes it, and I have come to treat it as non-negotiable: never worse than today.

Every AI capability gets a fallback to the behavior the machine already has. If the intelligence is unavailable, the existing logic runs. If the intelligence answers but is not confident enough, its answer is discarded and the existing logic runs. The rule table, the fixed alarm text, the manual procedure: all of it stays, and all of it stays in charge whenever the AI cannot show that it knows better.

This changes the conversation with the people who carry the risk. The question stops being “do you trust the AI?” It becomes “would you like the machine you have, plus help on the occasions when help is certain?” That is a much easier yes.

It also changes the engineering. Confidence has to be measured, and something other than the model has to judge it. The fallback has to be tested as seriously as the feature. And the team has to accept that the AI will sometimes be overruled by a decades-old lookup table, and that this is the system working.

AI should raise the ceiling of what a machine can do. It has no business touching the floor.
