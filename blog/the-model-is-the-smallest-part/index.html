The model is the smallest part

Industrial AI is mostly identity, authority, reliability and audit. The intelligence is the easy share, and it is becoming a commodity.

Sri Prasanna MG, November 11, 2026

Ask what it takes to put an AI agent to work next to a machine, and most people start with the model. Which one, how large, how fast, where it runs.

The more of my energy goes into agentic AI for the factory floor, the more that question shrinks. Choosing and running a capable model is the short chapter. The long chapters have older names.

Identity: who is this agent, and on whose behalf does it act? Authority: what is the most it may ever do to this machine, and who set that limit? Reliability: when the device restarts mid-task, does the agent finish its work without repeating an action? Audit: can someone reconstruct, a year later, what it did and why?

None of these are AI questions. They are the questions industry has always asked of people, contractors and software before letting them near production. An agent is simply the newest thing that has to answer them.

This has a strategic consequence that I think leaders underrate. Models, gateways and agent tooling are turning into commodities at remarkable speed. Whatever a company builds there, it will be able to buy better within a year. So adopt them, follow the open standards, and do not grow attached.

The part worth building is the part nobody can ship to you: the controlled way into your machines, the rules that encode what your machines may and may not do, the safe behavior when the AI is unsure, the record of every decision. That layer is made of domain knowledge. It is where decades of machine know-how turn into an advantage, instead of into a legacy.

An organization that spends its AI budget on the model is buying the cheapest component at the highest price. The ones that will lead are spending it on trust.
