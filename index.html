What I have come to believe about AI in industry

Six convictions that shape how I think about product, architecture and investment. Each one links to the longer argument.

  • Trust sets the pace

    Industry will adopt AI at the speed of trust, not at the speed of the models. Whoever makes agents accountable decides how fast that goes.

  • The model is the smallest part

    Identity, defined authority, reliability and audit are the larger share of the work, and the share that is hardest to copy.

  • Never worse than today

    When the AI is unsure or unavailable, the machine behaves exactly as it does now. AI raises the ceiling and leaves the floor alone.

  • Code owns the verdict

    A decision that matters belongs in something you can test and audit. Instructions to a model are not a control.

  • Local intelligence is ready

    Models that run next to the machine are already good enough for real operational work, and the data never leaves the site.

  • Simulation has to write rules

    A digital twin earns its cost when its answers become limits and decisions the real machine respects.

From the machine floor to the platform

I have worked every layer of the industry I now make product decisions for. That is why those decisions hold up in commissioning.

  1. 2009 to 2015

    The machine floor

    International Application Engineer, B&R

    Complex machine applications with OEMs and system integrators, advanced motion above all, from requirements through commissioning to series readiness.

  2. 2015 to 2021

    Quality and release

    Project Leader, ABB

    Owned test and release strategy for mechatronic software products. Learned that trust is built one release at a time.

  3. 2021 to 2024

    Platform and DevOps

    Product Owner, ABB

    Platform engineering and DevOps strategy across R&D teams, built on meaningful harmonization instead of forced standardization.

  4. 2024 to today

    Product

    Technical Product Manager

    Helped shape the vision for next-generation industrial software, and now lead its way from abstract idea to release-ready product.
