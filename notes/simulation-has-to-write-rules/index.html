Simulation has to write rules

A digital twin is worth its cost when its answers become limits the real machine respects.

Sri Prasanna MG, November 10, 2026

Digital twins are easy to admire and hard to justify. The rendering is impressive, and then the budget question arrives.

The answer I find convincing is narrow. Simulation pays when it answers an engineering question that would otherwise need a real machine at risk, and when that answer turns into something binding: a speed that may not be exceeded, a sequence that may not be skipped, a logic error fixed before commissioning.

Two disciplines keep it serious. An answer only counts once it stops changing as the simulation gets finer; before that it is a guess with good graphics. And a result that never changes a decision was entertainment.

This matters more as AI moves closer to machines. The twin becomes the place where an idea is tried first, and where the limits an AI must respect are found.
